# Full AI Disclosure — Cases in Practice

**Cases in Practice** was created by **Marc Watkins, Emily Donahue, and Danielle Clevenger** (University of Mississippi) and is presented by the **AI Institute** and the **Center for Excellence in Teaching and Learning (CETL)**. The authors directed the design, reviewed every screen, and take responsibility for its pedagogical framing.

**Human contribution.** The authors and workshop facilitators wrote the agenda, the nine case studies (three each on Designing Courses and Lesson Plans, Designing and Administering Assessments, and Grading and Feedback), and the four “questions to consider.”

**AI contribution.** Anthropic Claude (Fable 5.1, via Claude Code) built the site from the facilitators’ planning document on September 8, 2026: information architecture, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, the paper-and-motion visual system (adapted from the earlier *Teaching for Discernment* site), the landing-page card gallery (five SVG step illustrations drawn by Claude in the style of the *Teaching for Discernment* cards, whose WebGL card renderer is reused), the Talk / Type / Record response modes, the audio recorder and speech-transcription wiring, the segment timers, the draft discussion ground rules, the “principle seed” phrases, the facilitator notes, the accessibility statement draft, and the export engine (HTML download with embedded audio / print-to-PDF / copy). The looping tree-canopy video was AI-generated for *Teaching for Discernment* in August 2026 and is reused here.

**Limits.** Case studies and questions appear verbatim as written by the facilitators; AI did not alter them. Ground rules, principle seeds, facilitator notes, and the accessibility statement are AI-drafted suggestions the authors reviewed and may edit in `js/data.js`. AI output is not authorship or scholarship. Two cases cite external articles (Behind the Enemy Lines, Dec. 2025; EdSurge), which may change.

**Data.** This static site collects no data, uses no analytics, requires no accounts, and sends nothing to any server or AI system. Typed notes live only in the browser’s local storage and audio recordings only in the browser’s IndexedDB until exported or cleared.

**Speech recognition.** Optional transcription uses the speech-recognition service built into the attendee’s browser. In Chrome and Edge that audio is processed by Google’s or Microsoft’s speech servers while recording; Safari may process it on the device. The recording itself is never uploaded by this site, and transcription can be turned off (“Record audio only”).

**Brand.** Colors follow the University of Mississippi brand palette published on the UM Brand Portal; type is set in IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Serif, two of the university’s brand typefaces, under their open license. The licensed brand faces Matrole, Handelson One, and Termina are not embedded.

**Accessibility.** Designed to WCAG 2.1 AA (the standard adopted under ADA Title II). Automated axe-core checks pass with zero violations at desktop and mobile widths; see the Accessibility statement under “About” on the site for features, known limits, and how to report a barrier.
